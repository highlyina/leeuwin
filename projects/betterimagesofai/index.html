Better Images of AI

Image series created for Better Images of AI’s library.

Type “Artificial Intelligence” into your image search engine. Notice a pattern? Humanoid robots, glowing blue brains, outstretched hands, abstract circuitry… you get the idea. These images are misleading, unrealistic, and worst of all, they’re boring.

Better Images of AI is an organisation dedicated to this exact issue. For this project, I created an image series to submit to their library — an alternate repository for more realistic depictions of AI systems, applications and impacts. These images can be downloaded and used by anyone under a Creative Commons BY 4.0 license.

The series consists of three collage-based artworks that challenge typical AI imagery. Each piece explores a different aspect of AI and its sociocultural impacts: Agentic AI, companion chatbots, and the phenomenon of “AI slop”. Using visual metaphor inspired by academic discourse, historical art references and internet culture, the series aims to spark curiosity and critical reflection about the technology.

1 Pandemonium (Stochastic Flock)

This image builds on the “stochastic parrot” metaphor used to describe how language models mimic text patterns without real understanding. The “stochastic flock” extends this idea to Agentic AI: many, many stochastic parrots working together in a pandemonium of interconnected parts.

This piece shows the messy reality of how these systems function: not as a single ‘agent’ but as a complex network of models passing outputs between each other, often resulting in unpredictable outcomes. It questions what, exactly, we’re delegating our decisions to, and what the consequences might be.

2 Build-A-Soulmate

AI companion apps offer us intimacy on demand, free from the burdens of human relationships and tailored to our exact preferences. This image captures the unnatural process of constructing a partner in the same way we might customise a product.

It depicts fragments of classical sculptures — symbols of idealised beauty — being digitally selected and rearranged into a perfect ‘soulmate’. But the result is merely a flawed collection of parts chosen for their individual appeal, not a real person.

3 Slop Vanitas

This image is a satirical interpretation of “AI slop”: the low-quality, often nonsensical AI-generated content that floods our feeds. Inspired by traditional vanitas paintings — still life compositions that comment on the futility of pleasure and the certainty of death — this piece symbolises the inevitability of our content being scraped and regurgitated as slop.

The monitor displaying OpenAI’s logo alludes to the source of the problem, while the surrounding objects reference specific examples of “AI slop” and the various domains it can be found in: art, literature and music.
